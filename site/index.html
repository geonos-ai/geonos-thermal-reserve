03 The missing proof layer

From thermal inertia to a flexibility contract.

Generic demand-response platforms see a controllable load. Refrigeration controllers see a temperature setpoint. GEONOS translates between them—and refuses a dispatch when the operating evidence cannot support it.

01 / READ

Read the room

Power, room temperature, door events, throughput, compressor state and weather.

Existing telemetry · no new battery required
02 / LEARN

Map the envelope

Estimate safe kW, duration and rebound under the actual heat and operating state.

Facility-specific · uncertainty visible
03 / GATE

Protect the product

Check temperature, operations and operator rules before any plan can advance.

Hard safety gate · human approval
04 / PROVE

Verify the event

Compare the locked baseline with dispatch performance and temperature compliance.

Source-linked M&V · ready for settlement
CLAIM CONTROL

04 Why climate belongs in dispatch

The safe envelope changes as the day gets hotter.

A fixed flexibility promise is fragile. GEONOS conditions each plan on heat stress, operating load and product limits, so a facility can offer less capacity on the days when its thermal margin is already thin.

07 What $50,000 unlocks

One site. One supervised event. One result we can defend.

The prize would move GEONOS from a deterministic demonstration to field evidence—not fund a bigger dashboard. The pilot advances only when each data, safety and operating gate passes.

90-DAY FIELD PLAN
  1. 01
    Days 0–20 · Connect

    Data rights, equipment boundary, BMS/power connector and food-safety review.

  2. 02
    Days 21–45 · Observe

    Silent-mode modeling, holdout tests and operator review—no equipment control.

  3. 03
    Days 46–70 · Dispatch

    One supervised event with manual override, temperature hard stops and live logging.

  4. 04
    Days 71–90 · Verify

    Independent energy and temperature review, failure log and replication brief.

CURRENT STATUS · SEPTEMBER 2026

A working model, not a field claim.

This site is an interactive deterministic prototype built from public climate context and clearly labeled facility assumptions. GEONOS does not yet control equipment, use customer data, participate in an electricity market, or claim verified flexibility, savings or emissions reductions. That is exactly what the proposed pilot is designed to test.
