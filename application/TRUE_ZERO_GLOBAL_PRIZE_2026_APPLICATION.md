## 3. What is unique about your company? What does it do or will do to change its industry or how people do things?

**Paste-ready answer — under the 2,500-character limit:**

Cooling demand is growing just as power systems need more flexibility to integrate renewables and survive extreme heat. Cold-storage facilities already contain hours of thermal inertia, but most cannot offer it to the grid. Operators will not risk food or medicine, refrigeration controllers do not produce a market-ready flexibility commitment, and demand-response platforms cannot see each room’s changing thermal margin.

GEONOS closes that gap. Thermal Reserve converts existing 15-minute power, room temperature, door-opening, throughput, compressor-state and weather data into a facility-specific flexibility contract: how many kilowatts can move, for how long, with what rebound and uncertainty. Before any plan advances, it must pass hard product-temperature and operating rules and receive operator approval. After an event, GEONOS locks the baseline, measures delivered capacity and temperature compliance, and produces a source-linked M&V packet for the facility and its utility or aggregator.

The innovation is not load shifting by itself. It is the safety and verification layer between refrigeration controls and energy markets. GEONOS starts with brownfield telemetry and existing thermal mass rather than requiring a new battery or proprietary control system. A fixed flexibility promise becomes climate-aware: the platform offers less capacity when heat, throughput or door activity has already reduced the room’s margin.

Our first buyer is a multi-site cold-chain operator; aggregators, ESCOs and refrigeration service firms are channel partners. Revenue begins with a fixed-fee flexibility audit, followed by an annual site license and, where market rules permit, a share of verified grid or bill value. The same evidence model can extend from cold storage to food processing and retail refrigeration, while country packs replace local weather, tariff, market and safety rules.

Today we have an interactive deterministic prototype using public climate context and clearly labeled facility assumptions. It does not yet control equipment, use customer data, participate in an electricity market, or claim field-verified flexibility or emissions reductions. The next milestone is a 90-day Türkiye pilot: silent-mode calibration, operator and food-safety review, one supervised dispatch with hard stops, and independent energy and temperature verification.

## 13. Why should we choose your company?

Choose GEONOS because we turn an overlooked physical asset into measurable climate infrastructure without asking the customer to begin with a new battery.

The climate case is unusually direct. Cooling is growing with heat; renewable grids need flexible demand; and cold-chain operators cannot trade product safety for grid value. Thermal Reserve sits at that exact intersection. It makes the operating constraint the product—not an afterthought—and reports impact in hard quantities: qualified kW, duration, shifted and rebound kWh, dispatch success, uncertainty and temperature-limit breaches. Emissions are calculated only when an approved time-varying grid signal supports the claim; shifted energy is never mislabeled as energy saved.

The prototype is also honest about its stage. It works as an interactive deterministic model today, but it is not presented as customer traction, autonomous control or verified climate impact. Our next milestone is specific and falsifiable: within 90 days of securing a qualified host, complete silent calibration, pass refrigeration and food-safety review, run one supervised event with manual override and hard stops, and publish an independently reviewed result or an explicit no-go. Failure and inconclusive outcomes remain in the record.

The $50,000 award is catalytic because every dollar is tied to crossing that evidence gap: $12,000 for the site data/BMS connector, $11,000 for refrigeration and safety engineering, $10,000 for metering and the supervised dispatch, $10,000 for independent M&V, $5,000 for the Türkiye market and legal pack, and $2,000 contingency.

COP31 calls for efficiency and sustainable cooling as well as grids, storage and flexibility. GEONOS connects those agendas in one implementation-ready pilot. We are not asking the judges to believe an avoided-emissions claim. We are asking them to help create the first result that deserves one.
