# GEONOS Thermal Reserve — Prototype Model Card

## Purpose

This repository demonstrates a decision flow for refrigeration flexibility:

1. form a baseline load profile,
2. pre-cool before a grid event,
3. reduce load during the event,
4. model controlled recovery,
5. reject a plan that crosses the selected product-temperature limit, and
6. report qualified kW, shifted kWh, duration and rebound separately.

It is a product interaction and evidence-design prototype, not an engineering-grade thermal model or a dispatch controller.

## Data classes

| Label | Meaning in this repository |
|---|---|
| `PUBLIC CLIMATE` | NASA POWER MERRA-2 grid-cell values used for regional screening |
| `DEMO ASSET` | Transparent, deterministic facility assumptions; no customer records |
| `MODELED OUTPUT` | Results generated by the browser simulation; no field verification |

Public reanalysis cannot replace a facility sensor for dispatch qualification or settlement.

## Current demonstration logic

The browser model generates a 24-hour refrigeration baseline from:

- selected connected refrigeration load,
- a fixed normalized daily load shape,
- a visible outdoor-heat adjustment, and
- a product-specific thermal-inertia coefficient.

The dispatch plan then applies:

- a three-hour pre-cooling ramp before the selected event,
- the requested load reduction during the event,
- a two-hour recovery period after the event, and
- a heat-adjusted rebound ratio.

The model caps the qualified curtailment fraction using the product profile and outdoor temperature. If the requested fraction exceeds that modeled allowance or the projected temperature exceeds the product limit, the interface returns `PLAN REJECTED — SAFETY GATE`.

These formulas are intentionally deterministic and visible in `site/app.js`. They have not been calibrated to a real cold room.

## Intended field method

A field pilot would not reuse the demonstration coefficients. It would define, review and lock the following before an outcome is known:

- asset and meter boundary,
- room/product temperature boundary,
- healthy baseline period,
- weather and operating variables,
- missing-data and exclusion rules,
- event window and manual-override procedure,
- product-safety hard stops,
- model-quality thresholds,
- acceptable dispatch tolerance, and
- independent reviewer and approval roles.

For each interval `t`, delivered flexibility would be evaluated as the difference between the locked counterfactual baseline and observed event load, with uncertainty and exclusions retained. Event energy and post-event rebound would be reported separately. A result would be inconclusive—not forced positive—when data quality, operating comparability, baseline quality or safety evidence fails.

## Emissions discipline

Shifted energy is not automatically energy saved and is not automatically an emissions reduction. A carbon result would require an approved time-varying emissions signal and a documented calculation of the before/after load profile. Annual average grid factors are not sufficient to claim that a specific dispatch avoided emissions.

## Stop conditions

The proposed field workflow stops or reverts to silent mode when any of the following occurs:

- temperature limit or food-safety rule is crossed,
- qualified site personnel reject the plan,
- sensor, meter or control data are unreliable,
- the baseline cannot explain healthy operation within the pre-agreed threshold,
- manual override is unavailable,
- a controls or cybersecurity review fails, or
- the market program cannot accept the proposed measurement boundary.

## What this prototype does not claim

- live equipment control,
- autonomous AI control,
- customer or partner deployment,
- market qualification,
- energy savings,
- avoided emissions,
- food-safety certification, or
- field-verified flexibility.
